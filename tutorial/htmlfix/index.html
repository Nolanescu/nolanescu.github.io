Mission: Fix Your Homepage for Phones

Making Your Site Look Great on Mobile

Right now, your root homepage (index.html + styles.css) looks awesome on a big computer screen, but gets squished and cut off on a phone. Instead of rewriting everything, we're going to keep your Desktop version untouched and add a Mobile version right inside styles.css.

Rule of this mission: No giant copy-paste! Each step tells you where to look, what CSS property to change, and why it works so you build it by hand.

Before You Start: How to Test Like a Pro

  1. Open your root index.html and styles.css in your code editor.
  2. Open your site in Chrome or Edge and press F12 (or right-click → Inspect).
  3. Click the Phone / Tablet icon (Toggle Device Toolbar, or press Ctrl+Shift+M) and pick a phone width like 390px.
  4. Notice how you have to scroll sideways and the cards go off the screen. That's what we're fixing!
Step 1

Fix the CSS Box Math (box-sizing)

What's wrong: At the top of styles.css, your * rule resets border, padding, and margin to 0, but leaves out box-sizing. By default, CSS adds padding and borders on top of an element's width. So a 180px .item card with 16px padding and a 2px border actually becomes 216px wide!

Why our fix works: Setting box-sizing to border-box tells the browser: "Keep padding and borders inside the width I asked for." That way, later when we say width: 100% on mobile, it will fit 100% without poking out the sides.

Your task in styles.css:
  • Find the * selector at the very top of the file.
  • Add the box-sizing property with the value border-box.
Step 2

Create Your Mobile Section (@media Query)

What's wrong: Right now, every rule in styles.css applies to all screen sizes — huge monitors and tiny phones alike.

Why our fix works: A @media query lets you create a second set of rules that only turns on when the screen is narrow (for example, 768px or smaller). Everything above it stays your Desktop version, and everything inside it becomes your Mobile version.

Your task in styles.css:
  • Scroll to the very bottom of styles.css.
  • Create an @media block for screens with max-width: 768px:
@media (max-width: 768px) {
  /* Your mobile rules from Steps 3–7 go inside here! */
}
Step 3

Fix body Spacing & Extra Scroll

What's wrong: On desktop, body has margin-top: 40px and padding: 0px 40px. On a 390px phone, 40px padding on both sides steals 80px of space! Also, margin-top: 40px combined with min-height: 100vh makes the page 100vh + 40px tall, creating a tiny unnecessary scrollbar.

Why our fix works: Inside the mobile block, setting margin-top to 0 removes the extra height, and using smaller padding (like 24px top/bottom and 16px left/right) gives your text plenty of breathing room without wasting phone width.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add a body rule.
  • Set margin-top to 0.
  • Set padding to 24px 16px.
Step 4

Unlock the #header Width

What's wrong: Your #header container has width: 540px. A phone screen is usually only 360px to 430px wide, so a 540px box forces the whole page to overflow sideways.

Why our fix works: Changing width to 100% on mobile tells the header to match whatever phone screen is viewing it, instead of demanding 540 pixels.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add a #header rule.
  • Set its width property to 100%.
Step 5

Scale Down the Giant h1 Title

What's wrong: Your h1 ("Hi, I'm Nolan.") has font-size: 80px. That looks bold and awesome on a desktop monitor, but on a phone it's way too big and takes up half the screen.

Why our fix works: Dropping font-size to around 44px on mobile keeps it big enough to feel like a main title, while fitting cleanly on 1 or 2 lines.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add an h1 rule.
  • Set font-size to 44px (or test between 40px and 48px to see what looks best!).
Step 6

Stack the Passion Cards (.row & .item)

What's wrong: .row uses display: flex, which lines up Basketball, Chess, and Coding side-by-side in a horizontal row. Since each .item is 180px wide, three cards side-by-side need ~680px and shoot right off the edge of a phone!

Why our fix works: In Flexbox, changing flex-direction from its default (row) to column stacks the cards vertically one below another. Then setting .item's width to 100% makes each card stretch neatly across the phone screen.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add a .row rule and set flex-direction to column.
  • Add an .item rule and set width to 100%.
Step 7

Keep the Background & Text Readable

What's wrong: Your body uses background-position: right bottom and background-size: cover. On a tall portrait phone, that zooms tightly into the bottom-right corner of nolanbg.jpg. Also, light grey text (rgb(200, 200, 200)) can become hard to read if it lands over brighter parts of the photo.

Why our fix works: Centering the background (background-position: center) frames the photo better on tall screens. And giving .item or #header a slightly darker translucent background (or brightening body text color to #f0f4f8) makes sure every word pops clearly.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • In your mobile body rule, set background-position to center and color to #f0f4f8.
  • In your mobile .item rule, try a slightly darker background like rgba(15, 17, 23, 0.55) so the card text is super easy to read.
