Mission: Fix Your Homepage for Desktop & Mobile

Making Your Site Look Great on Every Screen

When your site was tested on a big 2K/4K monitor and on a phone, two different things broke: on a big monitor, everything stayed tiny in the top-left corner with a weird scrollbar; on a phone, boxes spilled off the screen. We're going to fix Part 1: Desktop first, then add Part 2: Mobile right inside styles.css.

Rule of this mission: No giant copy-paste! Each step tells you where to look, what CSS property to change, and why it works so you build it by hand.

Before You Start: How to Test Like a Pro

  1. Open your root index.html and styles.css in your code editor.
  2. Open your site in Chrome or Edge and press F12 (or right-click → Inspect).
  3. Click the Phone / Tablet icon (Toggle Device Toolbar, or press Ctrl+Shift+M).
  4. Use the width box at the top to test 3 screen sizes: a phone (390px), a laptop (1280px), and a big monitor (2560px — you can zoom out or type 2560 in Responsive mode!).
Part 1

Fix the Desktop Version (Laptop & 2K/4K Monitors)

What Looked Weird on the Big 2K/4K Monitor?

  • A mystery scrollbar on an almost-empty screen: Even with tons of empty space, a vertical scrollbar appeared on the right edge.
  • Everything tiny in the top-left corner: Your photo filled the right side nicely, while all the text and cards stayed crammed in the top-left corner.
  • Misaligned edges: "My passions" was pushed 16px right of "Hi, I'm Nolan.", and the 3 passion cards stuck out wider than the purple .highlight bar above them.
Desktop 1

Fix Box Math & Kill the 40px Scrollbar (* & body)

What's wrong: Two things cause trouble here. First, * is missing box-sizing, so CSS adds padding and borders on top of widths. Second, body has both min-height: 100vh (100% of screen height) AND margin-top: 40px. Adding 40px outside a 100vh box makes the page 100vh + 40px tall — forcing a scrollbar on every monitor, even 4K!

Why our fix works: Setting box-sizing: border-box on * keeps padding inside widths. Then moving that 40px from margin-top into padding on body keeps the spacing inside 100vh, so the scrollbar vanishes.

Your task at the top of styles.css:
  • Inside *, add box-sizing with value border-box.
  • Inside body, remove margin-top: 40px; and change padding from 0px 40px to 40px 56px.
Desktop 2

Center Content Vertically on Big Monitors (body)

What's wrong: On a tall 2K or 4K screen, your content sits glued to the very top-left corner while the whole bottom half of the left side is empty.

Why our fix works: Making body a vertical flex container with justify-content: center centers your header and passion cards vertically on the left side, balancing them nicely next to your photo on the right.

Your task in body (styles.css):
  • Add display with value flex.
  • Add flex-direction with value column.
  • Add justify-content with value center.
Desktop 3

Line Up #header & #passions Without Touching Your Photo

What's wrong: #header is 540px wide, while the 3 .item cards (`180px` + padding + border each) add up to 680px! Plus #header p and #passions both have padding: 16px on all sides, shifting them 16px inward from h1 and .highlight.

Why our fix works: Using width: min(680px, 52vw) on both #header and #passions gives them the exact same width, keeps them on the left 52% of the screen (never covering your face on a 1080p laptop!), and lets them grow up to 680px on 2K/4K. Changing .item from fixed width: 180px to flex: 1 makes the 3 cards share that exact width evenly.

Your task in styles.css:
  • On #header, replace width: 540px; with width: min(680px, 52vw);.
  • On #header p, change padding: 16px; to padding: 16px 0; so its left edge lines up with h1.
  • On #passions, add width: min(680px, 52vw); and change padding: 16px; to padding: 20px 0;.
  • On .item, remove width: 180px; and add flex: 1; instead.
Desktop 4

Scale the Title Smoothly (h1)

What's wrong: h1 is locked to 80px. On a smaller laptop window that can feel cramped, while on a 4K monitor it looks fine.

Why our fix works: CSS clamp(min, preferred, max) lets a font size grow smoothly with screen width (5vw) while staying between a minimum (48px) and a maximum (84px).

Your task in styles.css:
  • On h1, change font-size: 80px; to font-size: clamp(48px, 5vw, 84px);.
Part 2

Add the Mobile Version (@media Query)

Mobile 1

Create Your Mobile Section (@media Query)

What's wrong: Right now, every rule in styles.css applies to all screen sizes — huge monitors and tiny phones alike.

Why our fix works: A @media query lets you create a second set of rules that only turns on when the screen is narrow (for example, 768px or smaller). Everything above it stays your Desktop version, and everything inside it becomes your Mobile version.

Your task in styles.css:
  • Scroll to the very bottom of styles.css.
  • Create an @media block for screens with max-width: 768px:
@media (max-width: 768px) {
  /* Your mobile rules from Mobile 2–6 go inside here! */
}
Mobile 2

Adjust body Padding & Alignment on Phones

What's wrong: On a 390px phone, 56px left/right padding steals 112px of space! Also, on a tall phone where content scrolls, starting from the top looks cleaner than centering vertically.

Why our fix works: Inside the mobile block, setting smaller padding (24px 16px) and justify-content: flex-start gives your text plenty of room and starts cleanly near the top.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add a body rule.
  • Set padding to 24px 16px.
  • Set justify-content to flex-start.
Mobile 3

Let #header & #passions Use Full Phone Width

What's wrong: On desktop we used width: min(680px, 52vw) so content stays on the left half away from your photo. On a 390px phone, 52vw would only be ~200px wide — way too skinny!

Why our fix works: Changing width to 100% on mobile lets the header and passions section fill the full phone width cleanly.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add a rule for #header, #passions.
  • Set width to 100%.
Mobile 4

Scale Down the h1 Title for Phones

What's wrong: Even with clamp(), it's nice to pick a clean mobile title size so "Hi, I'm Nolan." fits comfortably on 1 or 2 lines.

Why our fix works: Setting font-size to around 42px on mobile keeps it bold without taking over the whole screen.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add an h1 rule.
  • Set font-size to 42px (or test between 38px and 46px!).
Mobile 5

Stack the Passion Cards (.row & .item)

What's wrong: .row uses display: flex, which lines up Basketball, Chess, and Coding side-by-side in a horizontal row. On a narrow phone, 3 columns side-by-side are way too squished.

Why our fix works: In Flexbox, changing flex-direction from its default (row) to column stacks the cards vertically one below another so each card gets the full width.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • Add a .row rule and set flex-direction to column.
Mobile 6

Keep the Background & Text Readable

What's wrong: Your body uses background-position: right bottom and background-size: cover. On a tall portrait phone, that zooms tightly into the bottom-right corner of nolanbg.jpg. Also, light grey text (rgb(200, 200, 200)) can become hard to read if it lands over brighter parts of the photo.

Why our fix works: Centering the background (background-position: center) frames the photo better on tall screens. And giving .item a slightly darker translucent background (plus brightening body text color to #f0f4f8) makes sure every word pops clearly.

Your task inside @media (max-width: 768px):
  • In your mobile body rule, set background-position to center and color to #f0f4f8.
  • Add an .item rule with background-color: rgba(15, 17, 23, 0.55); so the card text is super easy to read.
